Kumari Kandam explained in Tamil: is there a lost Tamil continent under the sea south of Kanyakumari? The legend comes from the commentary on Iraiyanar Akapporul (the first and second Tamil Sangams at Then Madurai and Kapatapuram, both taken by the sea) and from Silappathikaram (the Pahruli river and Kumari Kodu swallowed by the sea); the name Kumari Kandam appears in the 15th-century Kanda Puranam. In 1864 zoologist Philip Sclater proposed a sunken land, Lemuria, to explain lemur-like fossils in Madagascar and India, and Lemuria became linked with Kumari Kandam. Plate tectonics later showed India and Madagascar were joined and drifted apart about 90 million years ago, so no sunken continent is needed, and there is no evidence of one in the deep Indian Ocean. The real story: in the last ice age, about 20,000 years ago, sea level was about 120 m lower, India and Sri Lanka were joined by land across the Palk Strait, and rising seas drowned it about 7,000 years ago. The sea has kept changing the Tamil coast – Poompuhar in Manimekalai, Dhanushkodi in the 1964 cyclone, and the Saluvankuppam temple near Mamallapuram revealed by the 2004 tsunami.
